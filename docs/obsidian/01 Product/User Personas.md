# User Personas

Frontline is designed for veterans in stressful, time-pressured situations — and the people helping
them. Personas keep the design honest: plain language, low cognitive load, obvious next steps.

## 1. Marcus — needs help today (primary)

- **Who:** 34, post-9/11 Army veteran, recently lost hours at work, low on food this week.
- **Context:** On his phone, standing up, limited patience, some anxiety about "asking for
  handouts." Not sure what he qualifies for.
- **Needs:** The nearest food resource open **today**, whether he can walk in, what to bring, and
  what to say when he calls.
- **Frontline serves him with:** the guided **Food** flow → nearby pantries on a map/list → a
  detail page with hours + documents + **next steps** → a short **call script** he can read aloud.
- **Design implications:** urgency-first, "open now" clarity, big Call/Directions buttons, a nervous
  user script variant.

## 2. Denise — planning ahead / understanding benefits

- **Who:** 52, Navy veteran, stable housing, wants to use education and disability benefits she's
  never fully understood.
- **Context:** At home, willing to read a little, overwhelmed by VA terminology and PDFs.
- **Needs:** Plain-English explanations of **what she may qualify for**, what documents matter, and
  what to do next — without a determination she can't trust.
- **Frontline serves her with:** the **General VA benefits** / **Education** flows → benefit summary
  cards → the **AI summarizer** (what it is, who qualifies, documents, deadlines, next step) with
  honest hedging and official links. She **saves** benefits and checklists.
- **Design implications:** summarization quality, "you *may* qualify" language, save-for-later.

## 3. Alicia — caregiver / family member (acting on behalf)

- **Who:** 41, spouse of a disabled veteran, coordinating housing and healthcare help.
- **Context:** Juggling appointments, doing the legwork her partner can't.
- **Needs:** Trustworthy local resources across several categories, saved and organized, with clear
  contact info and what to prepare.
- **Frontline serves her with:** multi-category discovery, **saved resources**, document checklists,
  and the caregiver-relevant profile option. She relies heavily on **source + last-checked** trust
  signals.
- **Design implications:** saved organization, trust surfacing, family/caregiver profile type.

## 4. Ray — low trust, low tech-comfort

- **Who:** 68, Vietnam-era veteran, skeptical of apps and of being "signed up for something."
- **Context:** Worried about scams and privacy; will abandon anything that asks for SSN or a login.
- **Needs:** Immediate usefulness with **no account**, no sensitive data, and a clear statement that
  this isn't the government collecting his info.
- **Frontline serves him with:** demo-friendly, no-login access; **optional-only** profile; explicit
  "Frontline is a navigator, not the VA" and privacy messaging; large text and simple screens.
- **Design implications:** no forced signup, data minimization, accessibility, trust/disclaimer copy.

## Design takeaways (all personas)

- Lead with **"what do you need help with?"**, not a feature tour.
- Make **urgency** and **open-now** legible; make **Call/Directions** effortless.
- Keep AI output **honest and hedged**; always show **source + last-checked**.
- Never require sensitive data; every profile field is optional.
